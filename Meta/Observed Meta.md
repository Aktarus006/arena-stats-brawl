# Observed Competitive Brawl Meta

This note aggregates opponents encountered across tracked decks. It should not be treated as a statistically representative Arena metagame until the sample becomes much larger.

## Recurring themes from the Squall sample — 59 games

### Commander-centric engines
Many opponents rely on resolving or repeatedly deploying their commander. Drannith Magistrate, Disruptor Flute and edict effects have repeatedly performed well. Recent examples include Ketramose, Narset, Sephiroth, Gut, Oura, Esika and Tymna/Thrasios.

### Artifact / enchantment engines
A substantial number of difficult games have involved noncreature permanents generating repeated value: Paradox Engine, Forsaken Monument, equipment engines, Omniscience, Bitterblossom-style engines and artifact shells. Broad permanent interaction is therefore more valuable than creature-only removal in the observed field.

### Graveyard pressure / hate
Graveyard recursion is common, but so is graveyard denial. G57 vs Oura added a notable example: **Valgavoth shut off Squall's graveyard access**, forcing a pivot away from recursion. This reinforces the value of decks having a non-graveyard win path.

### Graveyard / land recursion
Seen through Slimefoot and Squee, Leovold, Lumra, Grist and Ketramose. Recurring fetchlands, Strip Mine, creatures and toolbox pieces remains a significant axis of play. G59 also showed opposing Reanimate on Tymna, reinforcing how often graveyards function as an active resource on both sides.

### Explosive green ramp / landfall
Tifa, Mythweaver Poq, Lumra, Primeval Titan/Grist and Golos-style lines demonstrate that green decks can move from parity to overwhelming board/mana states very quickly.

### Fast aggro
Merry, Hawkeye, Iron Man and Gut show that early board pressure remains common enough to justify cheap interaction and sweepers. Cori-Steel Cutter was particularly impactful in G56.

### Flash / evasive tempo
Dimir flash/Faerie strategies such as Oura can extend games while attacking on evasive axes. Commander denial helps, but long races can hinge on lifelink and evasion rather than pure graveyard value.

### Partner midrange / control
G59 vs Tymna/Thrasios added a representative high-interaction partner shell: **Force of Negation, Toxic Deluge, Dismember and Reanimate** all appeared in the same game. These decks can trade efficiently and rebuild, so recursive toolbox engines and diversified interaction are valuable.

### 5C value/control
Hei Bai, Omnath, Atraxa and Esika/Bridge variants can chain multiple engines and premium interaction. Early disruption is important before their card-advantage layer becomes overwhelming. G58 demonstrated that attacking mana rocks/setup before Bridge resolves can end the game before the engine starts.

### Free/cheap permission
Force of Will, Force of Negation, Fierce Guardianship, Daze, Flare of Denial, Mana Drain and Mana Leak have repeatedly disrupted Squall's attempts to stabilize or establish its commander. Protected-turn effects and discard remain important alongside removal. G59 again featured Force of Negation on a T1 Thoughtseize.

## Cards/engines repeatedly relevant on the opposing side

- Strip Mine / land denial
- Free or highly efficient counterspells
- Graveyard recursion engines / Reanimate
- Graveyard shutdown such as Valgavoth
- Primeval Titan / large ramp payoffs
- Artifact and enchantment value engines
- Commander protection effects such as Giver of Runes
- Sweepers such as Toxic Deluge that can reset Squall's board
- Partner commanders/value engines that can be redeployed repeatedly
