# Game 40 — Golos

## Result
Loss.

## Decisive sequence
An early **Lumra** accelerated the opponent dramatically and put the Golos deck too far ahead on resources.

Squall attempted to contain the board by exiling individual creatures, but the opponent's mana advantage continued to convert into threats. By around turn 5, a **9/9** was already on the battlefield and the deck could no longer recover the tempo/resource gap.

## Diagnosis
This is another example where efficient one-for-one creature interaction was insufficient once a ramp engine had already created a large mana advantage. The core problem was not inability to answer one specific creature, but failure to prevent or punish the early ramp snowball.

## Deckbuilding signal
At the 40-game checkpoint, high-mana/ramp strategies have repeatedly been difficult when their acceleration survives the opening turns. This reinforces the importance of **Strip Mine recursion, early discard, Drannith Magistrate, Disruptor Flute, and interaction aimed at mana engines** rather than simply increasing creature-removal density.
