# Game 46 — Urza, Lord High Artificer

## Result
Loss.

## Development
**Disruptor Flute** again successfully slowed the opposing commander plan. Squall also answered individual supporting pieces, including **Sai, Master Thopterist**.

However, **Paradox Engine** combined with the opponent's large artifact infrastructure generated far more mana/value than Squall could contain. Removing individual payoff creatures delayed the engine but did not dismantle the underlying artifact network.

## Diagnosis
This is another important distinction between **disrupting a commander** and **disrupting the deck's engine**. Disruptor Flute did its job, and individual removal also did its job, but Urza's artifact shell retained enough independent infrastructure to snowball through Paradox Engine.

The game reinforces the recurring artifact/permanent-engine weakness already observed against Thor, Ugin, Iron Man and other engine decks. The highest-value interaction is often the spell that removes the engine permanent itself rather than another creature.
